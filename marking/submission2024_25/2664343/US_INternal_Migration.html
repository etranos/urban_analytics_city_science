Introduction

Over the past century, the United States (US) internal migration has been studied as a significant indicator of demographic and economic changes (Greenwood, 1975). Many studies discussed the economic changes which are recognised as pull and push factors affecting the dynamic of internal migration. For example, employment opportunities in the destination county factor is found as the pull factor, while unemployment and higher housing costs in the origin county is identified as push factors (Molloy et al. 2011; Plane and Rogerson 1991). A temporal analysis of migration flow with those factors provides valuable insight into how the correlation evolves (Clark and Maas, 2015). Therefore, this project aims to conduct a temporal analysis of the effect of economic factors on the internal migration flow. The economic factors which will be studied include median income, employment, unemployment and median house value. The main objective is to identify the correlation between each of those economic factors and the internal migration flow and monitor how these correlations evolve over time.

According to Tranos et al (2015), gravitational models are widely utilised in the migration flow context especially used to study the spatial interaction between origins and destinations. This spatial interaction is represented by forces affecting the migration flow between the origin county and the destination county (Ramos, 2016). For example, the push factors represented by the attraction force affect the migration flow from the origin counties to the destination counties. On the other hand, the pull factors represented by the attraction force affect the migration flow from the destination counties to the origin counties. These forces are determined by coefficient values which are derived from the flow estimation process to demonstrate the effect of the economic factors on the migration flow. In addition, plotting the independent variables with the migration flow can indicate sometimes the type of relationship.

This study utilised three different models to estimate the coefficient which are Log-liner regression (OLS), Poisson regression and Negative Binomial regression. The coefficient in OLS represents the average change in the migration flow (dependant variable) with the corresponding change in the economic factors (independent variables). But in both Poisson and Negative Binomial regression models the coefficient represents the logarithm of the change ratio in the migration flow (dependent variable) with the corresponding change in the economic factors (independent variables) (Ramos, 2016). The evolvements of the coefficient estimate over time reveal an indicator of changes in the correlation between the dependent variable (migration flow) with the independent variables (economic factors). For example, Los Angeles (LA) County was recognised as an urban hub and migrant gateway based on the historical analysis of migration flow with the changes in labor demand (clark and maas, 2015). However, challenges of higher housing values and income inequality have influenced the migration flows toward LA county (Stoper & Manville, 2006; Sassen, 2016). Therefore, one of the objectives of this study is to investigate the temporal correlation between migration flow to LA with the economic factors (independent variables). Furthermore, this study will predict the migration flow within the US and the migration to LA based on four years of data.

The data utilised in this study is from the United States Bureau Census as it was downloaded by API in county-to-county geographic format for 2016, 2017, 2018 and 2019 years as for both the migration flows and push and pull variables which are origin population, number of the labor force, number of employees, number of an employed, and median house value. The study will start by preparing this data for the analysis stage for both internal migration flow and migration flow to LA, then showing some insights about the relationships with the variables by plotting them for both migration flows. Next, estimate the coefficients using the three models and using machine learning to predict the migration flows. The results of the coefficients will be analysed and discussed along with an evaluation of the three models and prediction models.

Data and Methodology

This study used data from the American Community Survey (ACS) Migration Flow data which illustrates the movement of people between different geographic areas inside and outside the United States over a year. The (ACS) Migration Flow data is collected through online surveys, mail questionnaires, and phone interviews. The data collection survey is conducted annually from 2005. However, after 2019 the data collection was affected by the COVID-19 pandemic because of many changes and relocation of living situations and jobs, thus lower responses and participation to the survey. Therefore, ACS made an adjustment to the data from 2020 onward, whereas this study used only data from 2016 to 2019 to avoid the effect of adjustment. Moreover, county-to-county flow was utilised in this study rather than between minor civil divisions or Metropolitan statistical areas due to the geographic consistency of counties.

Many R packages and libraries were utilised to achieve the objectives of this project including data acquisition, preparation, visualization and analysing. Data acquisition was conducted using tidycensus library to access the US Bureau Census website through a requested API token. In data preparation, many libraries were utilised such as tidyverse, dplyr and MASS to clean, transform and structure the data for visualisation and analyses. For data visualisation, ggplot2 and gridExtra libraries were used to plot the required graphs in the project. In analysing the data, boom and caret are utilised to model the data.

Analysis and Results

In this section, three different regression models were fitted to investigate the relationship between the dependent variable which represents the migration flow (MOVEDIN) and seven independent variables represent the economic factors: origin population, the distance between counties, median income, the total number of the labor force, total employees, and the total number of unemployed and median house value. The regression models utilised are Ordinary Least Squires (OLS), Poisson regression and Negative Binomial Generalised Linear Model (NB.GLM). These models were applied for the US internal migration flow for four years using datasets (data_16, data_17, data_18 and data_19) and also applied for migration to LA data.

Firstly, the OLS model is used to evaluate the linear relationships represented earlier between the dependent variable which represents the migration flow (MOVEDIN) and seven independent variables by transforming the dependent variable using the log() function and adding a small number which is here 0.5 to avoid the mathematical issue with log of zero. The independent variables were transformed as well using the log() function to ensure the migration flow values are positive to enhance the relationship linearity with predictions. The lm() function was used to fit the OLS model which reduced the the sum of squared residuals as the results of the coefficients with its standard error will be compared later with other models.

ols.model.16 <- lm(log(`MOVEDIN`+0.5) ~
                  log(POP1YR) + log(distanceC) + log(medianincomE)  + log(laborforceE) + log(employedE)  + log(UnemployedE) + log(medianhousevE), data = data_16)

ols.model.17 <- lm(log(`MOVEDIN`+0.5) ~
                  log(POP1YR) + log(distanceC) + log(medianincomE)  + log(laborforceE) + log(employedE)  + log(UnemployedE) + log(medianhousevE), data = data_17)

ols.model.18 <- lm(log(`MOVEDIN`+0.5) ~
                  log(POP1YR) + log(distanceC) + log(medianincomE)  + log(laborforceE) + log(employedE)  + log(UnemployedE) + log(medianhousevE), data = data_18)

ols.model.19 <- lm(log(`MOVEDIN`+0.5) ~
                  log(POP1YR) + log(distanceC) + log(medianincomE)  + log(laborforceE) + log(employedE)  + log(UnemployedE) + log(medianhousevE), data = data_19)

Secondly, the Poisson model is used due to its performance with a counted dependent variable as the migration flow is a count data which assumes the dependent variable has a Poisson disruption. Also, used by transforming the independent variables using the log() function, while not transforming the dependent variable. The log link function was used to fit the poisson model which ensures the predictors to be positive for multiplicative effect on the dependent variables.

Discussion

In this section, key outcomes of fitting the three regression models will be highlighted representing the coefficient between migration flow (MOVEDIN) and the seven independent variables with its standard error for the four years. The findings of both migration flows within the US and to LA will be discussed. Furthermore, the two trained models will be evaluated based on their predictions compared with the actual results.

The type of relationship between the dependent variable (migration flow) and the seven independent variables can be identified as either a positive or negative relationship. The positive relationship is indicated if the variable’s coefficient value is positive which represents a direct relationship with the dependent as the independent variable increases, the dependent variable (migration flow) increases. On the other hand, the negative correlation is identified if the coefficient value is negative which means an inverse relationship as the independent variable increases the dependent variable rises.

The first correlation of the internal US migration flow with the independent variable will be discussed is the population of the origin, that identified to be a positive relationship with the internal migration flow for the four years as the best coefficient was with Poisson model based on the lower standard error. The value of the coefficient was around 0.64 which represents the logarithm of the rate ratio, thus in this case equals 89.6% meaning that if the origin population of a county increase by one unit, the dependent variable (migration flow) rise by approximately 89.6%.

A negative relationship with the distance variable was identified, as the best coefficient value was achieved also by the Poisson model by around -1. This means that the logarithm of the rate ratio is equal to 63.2%. Similarly, a negative correlation with the median income variable was identified with a best coefficient value by Poisson model of around -0.6 over the four years. This means that the logarithm of the rate ratio is equal to roughly 54% meaning that if the median income in the origin county increases by one unit the migration flow decreases by 54%.

Employment factors are one of the main effects on the migration flow. A strong positive relationship was identified with the labor force variable with an approximate coefficient value of 4 in the four years. This means that if the total number of the labor force in a county increases by 1 unit, the migration flow inside the US increases by 4 units. Moreover, the relationship with the total number of employees was found to be negative by coefficient around -3 by Poisson and Negative Binomial in the four years. Furthermore, the correlation with the total unemployment variable was identified to be positive by the OLS model while negative by the Poisson and Negative Binomial models. Based on the very small standard errors presented by the Poisson model and this data is count-based, the coefficient from the Poisson model is selected to illustrate this relationship which is found to be around -0.5 which means that the logarithm of the rate ratio equals to roughly 54%. This indicates that if the total unemployment increased by 1 unit the migration flow decreases by 54%.

Housing affordability is one of the economic factors affecting the migration flow. The relationship between median house value and migration flow was found to be positive with the most precise estimated coefficient by the Poisson model increasing from 0.03 in 2016 reaching 0.13 in 2019. This means that the median house value increased by one unit the flow migration increased in 2016 by 3%, while in 2019 the migration increased by 13%.

The relationships between the migration flow to LA with the seven independent variables show a strong correlation, especially with the employment situation. For example, a positive relationship with the total labor force is identified by all models by around 9 for the four years. This means that the if total labor force of a county increases by one unit, the migration flow toward the LA increases by 9 units. Moreover, a negative relationship with the total employment was identified by all models for the four years with approximately -6. Additionally, a negative relationship with the total unemployed is identified by all models for the four years with around -0.3.

Furthermore, a negative relationship is identified with the median income variable by around -1.5 by all models. Similarly, a negative correlation is identified with the population of the origin by an estimated coefficient of around -1 by all models in the four years. The relationship between the median house value in the origin county with the migration flow to LA was positive by all models and increased from 0.9 in 2016 to 1.7 in 2019. This means that the median house value in the origin county increased by one unit, the flow towards LA increased by 0.9 in 2016 and 1.7 in 2019. In addition, the relationship with distance to LA as a negative value with the migration flow identified by all models around -1.2 all over the four years.

To evaluate the performance of the linear regression model and the generalized linear model, three key factors are considered which are RMSE, R-squared, and MAE. Based on the RMSE values which compute the average error between predicted and actual values, both models have similar RMSE values with a 1.5 difference, thus a slight advantage for the Poisson regression model. When considering R-squared values which represent the proportion of the variance in the (migration flow) dependent variable, both models have very low R-squared values with also a slight advantage for linear regression model. Furthermore, the MAE values are close for both. Both models perform well in their predictions, while Poisson regression model recognised to predict slightly more precisely.

Conclusion

In summary, this study has analysed the economic effects on the migration flow within the US and to LA including coefficient estimates, plotting the variables and predictive analysis. This was accomplished by following the stages of data preparation, variables plotting, data analysis and predictive. It is concluded that the pull economic factors based on the positive relationship with the flow migration are the number of labor force, median house value and origin populatoin. On the other hand, the push economic factors based on the negative relationship with the migration flow are the total number of employed, unemployed, median income and distance. The three models perform well in modeling the data, however the Poisson regression model fits the data more also predicts more precisely compared to the linear regression model.
